1.Why this document exists

HFFA leadership has asked every division to stop operating meeting to meeting and start operating like a war room: know our own challenges, set real goals tied to the statewide strategy, and go attack them. This is the Maui Division's answer.

It is built around three facts we do not get to argue with.

We are three people

That is the entire Maui Division board capacity. Three people cannot run eight goals. Three people can run three goals, one each, if nothing else is allowed onto the list. Every strategic plan that has ever failed in this union failed because it had more goals than owners.

We have two and a half years of runway, and a hard date

The agreement runs to June 30, 2029, and impasse is automatic on January 31, 2029. That is not downtime, it is the build window. Evidence, public support, and a working relationship with the department are the three things that cannot be manufactured once we are already in front of an arbitration panel. Two and a half years sounds like plenty. It is three budget cycles, and the last one is already too late to build in.

We are being priced against another department instead of against our own work

That is the real problem underneath the pay problem, and Section 3 is how we answer it.

Still pending: the pay schedule

Every salary figure in Section 4 rests on the old CBA plus the 3% across-the-board increase effective 7/1/25. A four-year award covering 2025 to 2029 almost certainly carries increases effective 7/1/2025, 7/1/2026, 7/1/2027 and 7/1/2028, and a published figure already puts 2.5% effective 7/1/2028 in that award.

There is very likely an increase that took effect on July 1, 2026, which would make $71,132, $101,278 and $24.43 all stale. Re-derive Section 4 from the award before any salary figure leaves the room. The 2,912-hour year, the step count, the 28-year ladder, the survey ranking and the cost of living indices are unaffected.

2.The one thing we are trying to do

By January 31, 2029, Maui County's own records, its own fire administration, and its own residents describe and defend the fire service on fire service terms, so that an arbitration panel reads it from the employer's side of the table and not just from ours.

Everything in this plan either serves that sentence or comes off the list.

Notice what it does not say. It does not say "win the wage adjustment." We do not win the contract in a division strategic plan, and pretending otherwise is how divisions end up with goals they cannot control. It says: change what the county believes about what we are worth, on the record, before we sit down. When we do sit down, we want the other side's own documents, own chief, and own voters already making our argument for us.

3.How we argue: Our Own Number

The problem, stated plainly

The employer has decided that the fire service does not carry the same financial value as police, and that we should always land below them. That is the mindset we are up against.

Here is the trap. The moment we answer with "police received X, we should receive X," we have agreed to three things without meaning to:

  1. That police compensation is the correct measuring stick for fire compensation.
  2. That we are asking for a share of what someone else was given, instead of the price of what we do.
  3. That the ceiling on our worth is set somewhere other than in our own work.

We lose that argument by entering it. We also hand the employer the easiest deflection in government: put two public safety unions in a room fighting over the same pot, and the employer never has to answer for the size of the pot.

The doctrine

We never argue for a share of theirs. We always argue for the price of ours.

  1. No Maui Division material ever prices firefighters against police. No parity language. No "police got." Not in testimony, not on social media, not in a comment section, not in anything county-facing. If a draft contains a police comparison, the draft is not finished. One forum is carved out of this rule by statute, below.
  2. We publicly support police compensation, and we mean it. A race to the bottom between public safety unions only ever serves the employer, and every hour a Maui police officer works is real work. It also removes the only counterattack available to anyone who wants to dismiss us, which is that we are just jealous of another department. You cannot run that play on a union that is on record supporting the other department's pay.
  3. Every number we produce is built from fire inputs. Our hours. Our step ladder. Our scope. Our recruitment market. Our cost of living. Our credentials. Our cost of failure. Police are never the source of the number.
  4. When they raise police, we name the move and pivot. Do not take the bait, do not get defensive.

"The county is pricing us against another department instead of against our own work. We support what our police officers earn. Here is what our work is worth."

Then give the fire number. Every time. The same way, by all three of us.

Where this doctrine stops: interest arbitration

Read this before adopting Rule 1

Firefighters cannot strike. Under HRS 89-11(e), BU-11 goes to binding interest arbitration. HRS 89-11(f) lists the factors the panel shall give weight to, and requires the panel to explain in writing how it took each one into account. Factor 6, word for word:

"Comparison of wages, hours, and conditions of employment of the employees involved in the arbitration proceeding with the wages, hours, and conditions of employment of other persons performing similar services, and of other state and county employees in Hawaii."

"Other state and county employees in Hawaii" includes police. The panel is required by statute to weigh that comparison whether we like it or not. A union that refuses to engage a mandatory statutory factor does not remove it from the case. It just leaves the employer to characterize it unopposed.

So the doctrine governs public, political, and county-facing communication, which is where the parity trap actually operates. It does not govern our advocacy before an arbitration panel.

And the statute hands us the framing in the same sentence. Factor 6 says "other persons performing similar services" first, and other state and county employees second. Persons performing similar services means other fire departments, which is our own evidence base. So in arbitration we lead factor 6 with the 37-department peer survey, on our terms, and address in-state comparisons second and on our own initiative. We do not let the employer define factor 6 as "police."

Two more things in the same statute every board member should know

  • HRS 89-11(f)(4) bars predicating the employer's ability to pay on the premise that it could raise taxes, fees, or charges. No Division material may ever imply it. It is a losing argument by statute.
  • HRS 89-11(g): an agreement reached by arbitration decision is not ratified by the employees. Members do not vote on it. That is a hard fact about where our leverage lives, and it means member mobilization must never be aimed at a ratification vote that will not happen. The leverage is the panel and the political environment the panel reads.

Why this is stronger than parity, not just safer

A parity argument caps us at whatever police received. An own-number argument has no ceiling except the truth about the job. It is also the only argument that survives in front of an arbitrator, a councilmember, and a homeowner at the same time, because it does not require any of them to take a side between two departments they both like.

On the $24.43

This is the number to lead with in public. A resident hears "$71,000," divides by a 2,080-hour office year, gets $34 an hour, and nobody in county government has ever corrected them. The correct divisor is in our contract. Twenty four dollars and forty three cents an hour, to run into the thing everyone else runs out of. It requires no comparison to any other department to land, which is exactly what makes it the right weapon under this doctrine.

On the 28 years

A firefighter hired at 25 reaches the top of the pay scale at 53. In the median department of a 37-department survey, that same firefighter tops out just past 31. We are not slow. We are last, with nobody behind us.

Use 6.5, not "about 6"

The median was recomputed across all 37 rows: median 6.5 years, mean 9.0, range 2.75 to 28.0. Rounding down to "about 6" shades the number in our favor, and shading in our own favor is exactly what gets a brief discredited. 28 against 6.5 is still more than four times longer, which is plenty.

One trap to avoid with the MIT figure

Compare it annually, not hourly. Our $24.43 is built on a 2,912-hour year. MIT's $58.53 is built on a standard 2,080-hour year. Putting those two hourly numbers side by side looks devastating and is methodologically wrong, and the first economist who reads it will say so. The honest and still very strong version is annual: top step $101,278 against a living wage of $121,742, which leaves a firefighter $20,464 short after 28 years on the job.

Index discipline

Under the housing-heavy C2ER index our pay sits at or below the 40th percentile after cost of living. Under the conservative federal BEA index it sits mid-distribution. Say "at or below the 40th percentile after cost of living, using the C2ER index," and name the index. Never say "lowest in the nation." The two indices diverge, someone will check, and one overstated claim costs us more credibility than the claim was ever going to buy.

Known but not yet sourced, and research not yet started

Do not publish until it is sourced

  • Applicant pool down roughly 73% over nine years. Currently a Maui Division estimate: a 2025 count from DPS records against a conservative 2017 baseline. DPS is past its legal UIPA deadline and still owes us the official 2017 figure. Until it lands, this number is attributed to the Maui Division and marked pending official data, or it does not go out. The stonewall is itself usable. See Goal 1, Action 1.3.
  • The Fire Fighter Trainee exam publishes no minimum passing score. We have one documented case of a candidate passing with a 54. HPD publishes a police minimum of 70. Maui PD does not publish its number. One data point is not a system. Do not present it as one until we have the distribution.

Research we have not started, and should

  • Run and scope data. What MFD actually responds to, by incident type: fire, EMS and first response, ocean rescue, hazmat, wildland, motor vehicle, technical rescue. The all-hazards scope is the foundation of the value argument and we do not have the numbers in hand.
  • ISO Public Protection Classification. Maui County's current rating, what drives it, and whether staffing affects it. Potentially the highest-value unexplored item in the plan, because ISO ratings feed homeowner and commercial insurance pricing, and post-2023 Maui insurance costs are already a live public grievance. If fire staffing measurably touches what residents pay for insurance, we have a constituency with a financial stake and not just a sentimental one. Verify before claiming anything.
  • Vacancy and overtime cost. What the county currently spends covering positions it has not filled. Underfunding a payroll is not free, and the county has never had to say out loud what it costs.
One rule about August 2023

The Maui Division does not use the Lahaina fire as a compensation argument. Ever.

Where official post-fire reports contain findings about capacity, staffing, or resources, we may cite the official finding, factually, in its own words, in the same tone we would cite any other government report. We do not narrate it, we do not claim ownership of the community's grief, and we do not build a slide around it.

This is first a matter of decency. It is also strategically correct, because the entire constituency we are trying to build in Goal 2 lives in that community, and any hint that we are leveraging what happened to them would end this plan in a week.

5.The three goals

Three people. Three goals. One owner each. Every goal has been run through the goal test in Appendix A and answers all seven questions, because a goal that cannot answer all seven is a wish with a deadline.

Why these three, and not three others

This is the part to lead with in front of the state E-Board, because it is the part that is not a matter of taste.

BU-11 money is ultimately decided by an arbitration panel weighing the ten factors in HRS 89-11(f), and the panel must explain in writing how it weighed each one. Each of the three pillars builds a named statutory factor.

PillarStatutory factors it builds
Value(6) comparison with persons performing similar services, (7) cost of living, (8) overall compensation received
Constituency(3) the interests and welfare of the public
Partnership(1) the lawful authority of the employer, (4) the financial ability of the employer. A department that has documented its own staffing and recruitment need in its own budget request makes an ability-to-pay defense much harder to run

These are not three good ideas. They are the three things a division can build in peacetime that a panel is legally obligated to weigh. Factor 3 in particular, the interests and welfare of the public, is the one no brief can manufacture at the last minute and the one a real constituency proves.

1Value

The county's own records price us on our own work

Owner: [OWNER 1]
What is different, and by when

By the close of the FY2028 budget cycle (June 10, 2027), at least four separate official County of Maui records contain a fire-derived compensation or capacity measure, entered by someone other than us. Qualifying: BFED testimony and reports, Fire Commission minutes, the department's own budget submission, Council floor debate, a councilmember's written communication.

Who has to act

BFED committee members, the Maui Fire and Public Safety Commission, the Managing Director, the Fire Chief.

Do not write names into this goal yet

All nine Council seats are on the ballot November 3, 2026. The current BFED chair is running for mayor and the Council Chair is stepping down, so the FY2028 budget will be reviewed by a council we have not met, chaired by someone not yet chosen. Names go in after the election. See Section 7.

What exactly we want them to do

Enter our numbers into their record and cite them as their own. A councilmember asks the 2,912-hour question on the record at a budget hearing. The Fire Commission takes up the recruitment pipeline as a named agenda item. The department's budget narrative uses the step-ladder finding.

What a firefighter gets

An arbitrator weighs an employer's own record far more heavily than a union's claim. Every county document carrying our number converts a union assertion into an employer admission, and those admissions are still sitting there when we open the contract. This is the goal that buys leverage we cannot buy any other way.

How we count it

Count of official county records containing a fire-derived measure. Baseline July 2026: 1 (the July 16, 2026 Fire Commission testimony). Target 4 by June 10, 2027. Verify quarterly against posted minutes and agendas.

What we stop doing

We stop producing any document that leads with a police comparison. We stop answering "police received X" with a parity ask.

Actions 1.1 to 1.5
  • 1.1 Build the all-hazards scope package. Request run data by incident type from the department first, under Goal 3. If that stalls past 60 days, file a public records request. Deliverable: a one-page scope sheet showing what a Maui firefighter is actually dispatched to.
  • 1.2 Build the ISO and insurance research memo. Establish whether Maui's ISO classification is affected by staffing, and whether that flows to resident insurance costs. Publish nothing until sourced. If it holds up, it becomes the spine of Goal 2.
  • 1.3 Escalate the DPS applicant data. The UIPA deadline has passed. File a formal appeal with the Office of Information Practices. Two outcomes, both useful: we get the official number, or we have a documented record of the county refusing to disclose its own firefighter recruitment data, which is a story that tells itself in front of the Fire Commission.
  • 1.4 Produce the standing Maui Division fact sheet. One page, verified table only, no police comparison, correct index disclosure, refreshed quarterly. Every board member carries it and every ally gets handed one.
  • 1.5 Testify at every Fire Commission meeting where the agenda touches staffing, recruitment, or capacity. Consistency of presence is what turns a union into a fixture the body expects to hear from.
2Constituency

We can put residents into the county record on demand

Owner: [OWNER 2]

First, the thing leadership was right to push back on. "Improve our public image" is not a goal. It cannot be counted, nobody has to do anything differently, and no firefighter gets anything from it. The question leadership asked is the correct one: do we want the public saying nice things about us, or do we want them going after their elected officials when we ask?

An audience is not a constituency. An audience watches you. A constituency does a specific thing when you ask, inside a specific window.

We currently have an audience. Everybody loves the fire department. That love has never once appeared in a county budget record, because nobody has ever asked it to do anything. Affection that has not been converted into action is worth exactly zero at a budget hearing, and we should stop counting it as an asset.

What is different, and by when

By the FY2028 BFED hearings (April 2027), the Maui Division can generate at least 100 pieces of resident written testimony supporting fire staffing and compensation within 14 days of asking, from a contact list we own.

Who has to act

Maui County residents on our list, plus organized allies: community and neighborhood associations, HOAs in wildfire-exposed areas, Realtors and insurance agents, hotel and resort risk managers, business associations, other labor including ILWU, churches and community groups.

What exactly we want them to do

Submit written testimony to BFED by name and district, via eComment or in person, during the FY2028 budget hearings. The ask ladder, cheapest to most expensive for them:

  1. Join the list (text or email, with consent)
  2. Submit written testimony via eComment (about 5 minutes, enters the permanent record)
  3. Testify in person at a district evening session (3 minutes, plus 1 to conclude)
  4. Contact their own councilmember directly
  5. Vote, and talk to their neighbors about it
What a firefighter gets

Staffing, and political cost attached to underfunding us. A councilmember who hears from 100 constituents by name and district about fire staffing behaves differently in April than one who hears from nobody. That behavior shows up in the budget, and the budget is where positions and premium pay live.

How we count it

(a) size of the owned list, (b) conversion rate when we make an ask, (c) count of resident testimonies in the county record. Baseline July 2026: list 0, conversion unknown, testimonies 0.

What we stop doing

We stop doing unmeasured goodwill. Every public touch either grows the list or it does not happen. Station tour, school visit, community event, social post, open house: all of them end with a capture, or we do not spend a shift on them.

Actions 2.1 to 2.6
  • 2.1 Stand up the list. One page, one form, consent captured, text-first because SMS turnout beats email turnout every time. QR code on everything physical. This is the single asset this pillar exists to build and it does not exist yet.
  • 2.2 Build the one-click testimony tool. Pre-filled eComment template, district lookup so a resident knows who their councilmember is, and a 3-minute in-person script. The gap between "I support the fire department" and testimony entering the record is almost entirely friction, and friction is a solvable engineering problem.
  • 2.3 Recruit organizational allies, not just individuals. Ten community associations with 200 members each beats a year of individual signups.
  • 2.4 Build the insurance-interest constituency, contingent on Action 1.2 holding up. Homeowners facing insurance increases, Realtors whose closings depend on insurability, and commercial risk managers all have a financial stake in fire capacity. The most durable constituency available to us.
  • 2.5 Run one live test well before it counts. Pick a low-stakes county item in late 2026 and make a real ask. Measure conversion. Finding out in April 2027 that the list does not convert is finding out too late.
  • 2.6 Content pipeline. Everything ends in the same call to action and drives to the same signup. Production without capture is decoration.
3Partnership

MFD carries a shared position into the FY2028 budget

Owner: [OWNER 3]

Where we actually stand. The relationship with department administration is workable but transactional. We deal on grievances, policy consultation, and R4R. There is no shared agenda. Partnership here is an upgrade play, not a repair job, and the gap is the ask, not the trust.

The trade. We do the work the department is not doing, which is the data, the public case, and the recruitment marketing. In return we ask for resources and a joint position. Their pain and our pain are the same pain: vacancies, overtime cost, Commission scrutiny, capacity expectations. Every one of those is solved by the same two things we want, which are competitive pay and full staffing.

What is different, and by when

By the department's FY2028 budget submission, MFD's budget request and the Chief's BFED testimony include recruitment and retention compensation language jointly developed with the Maui Division, and the department has committed identified resources to at least two Maui Division public initiatives.

Who has to act

The Fire Chief, the Deputy Chief, department administration. The Fire Commission is the venue where the ask becomes public if it is ignored in private.

What exactly we want them to do

Five items, delivered as one written ask so it can be answered as one:

  1. Budget lane. Jointly developed recruitment and retention compensation language in the FY2028 submission and in the Chief's testimony to BFED.
  2. Data. Release run volume by incident type, response times, vacancy counts by rank, applicant and exam counts by year, overtime hours and cost. Most of this the department already holds.
  3. Production resources. Apparatus and crew time, station access, permission to film, PIO cooperation, use of department name and imagery for a joint recruitment campaign. This is the concrete version of the commercials leadership raised. Do not ask for "support." Ask for a truck, a crew, four hours, and a sign-off.
  4. Joint appearances. Chief and Division Chair together at the Fire Commission and at BFED on recruitment and staffing.
  5. A standing quarterly labor and management strategic meeting, separate from grievance meetings. This is the structural fix. A relationship that only ever meets about grievances will stay transactional forever, because every conversation in it is adversarial by construction. One recurring meeting that is not about a grievance is what converts it.
What a firefighter gets

The employer's own department making our argument is worth more than any document we will ever write, because it cannot be dismissed as self-interest. Plus staffing, plus real production values on the public work, plus a channel that is not a grievance.

How we count it

(a) jointly developed language present or absent in the submission, (b) resource commitments made and honored, (c) joint appearances, (d) the quarterly meeting either exists on a calendar or it does not.

What we stop doing

We stop treating the department solely as a grievance counterparty. We stop letting them first see our public material in the news.

6.How partnership and accountability coexist

This plan asks us to build a partnership with the department while we are also on record before the Fire Commission making the case that the administration has historically sat out the budget lane that the police chief works every year.

Those are not incompatible, but they will read as either naive or hostile unless we hold both deliberately. Three rules, and they are binding.

  1. Accountability material addresses structures and records, never individuals. We talk about what the administration did or did not do in a budget hearing, and we cite the record. We do not talk about a person's character. A document that reads as "your chief failed" flips the entire room into defending him, and we lose a body we needed.
  2. When accountability material concerns the department, they see it before the public does. Not for approval. For notice. A department that first learns our position from the news will never give us a truck and a crew.
  3. Partnership asks go in private, in writing, with a specific list, and we keep the receipt. If the partnership route fails after a genuine and documented attempt, the accountability lane escalates, and we escalate with a paper trail showing we asked first. The board decides that escalation together. Nobody freelances into it.

7.The election sits in the middle of this plan

Every county official this plan targets is on the ballot on November 3, 2026. All nine Maui County Council seats, two-year terms, plus the mayor. The current BFED chair is running for mayor. The Council Chair is stepping down. The council that reviews the FY2028 budget in April 2027 is a council that does not exist yet.

The plan as first drafted named targets who will not be in those seats. That was an error, and correcting it exposes something better than the error: the Local's PAC endorsement operation and this strategic plan are the same fight, and right now they are being run as if they were unrelated.

The window

A newly elected official has no fixed position on firefighter compensation. That is the cheapest moment to set one that we will ever get, and it expires within weeks of them taking office.

The department's FY2028 budget request is assembled this fall. If our written partnership ask arrives after the Mayor transmits in March 2027, it is a full year late, and Goal 3 cannot be scored until FY2029.

9.How we work: the war room

A war room is not a mood. It is an operating rhythm with a live picture, decisions made in the room, and owners reporting a number.

The wall. One page, physical or digital, visible to all three. The objective, three goals, the number for each, the owner, current status, next action, blockers. Updated every meeting. If the wall is stale, the war room is not running.

Cadence. Ninety-minute board meeting monthly. Fifteen-minute check between the three weekly, by text or call. With three people, monthly alone is too slow, and the weekly check is what keeps a stalled goal from burning four weeks before anyone notices.

Rules of the room

  • Everything on the table stays in the room.
  • Disagree hard, leave aligned. Once the board decides, all three carry the same line outside.
  • No goal without one owner. Not a committee. One name.
  • If a number has not moved in two consecutive meetings, the goal gets rescued or killed. Out loud, on purpose, by decision. Goals do not get to die quietly.
  • Nothing gets added without something coming off. Three people, finite hours. Every yes is a no to something else, and we say the no out loud.

10.What Maui takes to the state E-Board

Four things, in order of how transferable they are.

  1. The goal test (Appendix A). Seven questions. The universal piece, costs nothing to adopt, and it kills "improve our public image" in the room instead of six months later. Any division can use it tomorrow.
  2. The war-room operating rhythm (Section 9). The fixed agenda, the wall, the kill rules. Scale-independent.
  3. Maui's plan as the worked example. Not as a template to copy, because each division's challenges are its own. As proof that the structure produces something concrete.
  4. One real statewide policy proposal, and this is the ask.

The no-police-comparison discipline only works if all four divisions and the state office hold it.

One division making a parity argument re-anchors the ceiling for every other division and for the state table. It is the kind of discipline that is worth nothing if it is 80% adopted. Maui is proposing it as a statewide communications standard, not as a Maui preference, and Maui is volunteering to hold it first and report on whether it works.

Propose it with the carve-out attached, not as an absolute

A blanket "never compare" adopted statewide would collide with HRS 89-11(f)(6), which makes that comparison a factor the arbitration panel must weigh and must explain in writing. The proposal is: hold the line absolutely in public, political, and employer-facing communication, and in arbitration lead factor 6 with peer fire departments so the employer never gets to define that factor for us. A state office that hears the carve-out proposed alongside the discipline will take the discipline more seriously, not less, because it shows we know which room we are in.

Appendix A. The goal test

A goal is not a goal until it answers all seven. If it cannot, it is a wish, and we either fix it or we do not adopt it.

  1. What is observably different in the world, and by when?
  2. Who has to decide or act differently? Name them.
  3. What exactly do we want them to do? An action, not a feeling.
  4. What does a firefighter get out of it? Traceable to pay, staffing, safety, or career.
  5. How do we count it? A number we can check without arguing about it.
  6. Who owns it? One name. Not a committee.
  7. What do we stop doing to make room for it?

Question 7 is the one nobody writes down and the one that decides whether a three-person board ships anything. A board that adds without subtracting is a board that will report the same status for eleven months.

Worked example of a failure

"Improve our public image." Question 1: no. Question 2: no. Question 3: no. Question 4: no. Question 5: no. It fails five of seven, and it is the goal every union in America has on a slide right now.

The fix is Goal 2. Same underlying instinct, run through the test until it became a thing three people can actually do and count.

Standing writing rules for everything this plan produces

  • No police comparison in anything public, political, or county-facing. Carve-out: interest arbitration, where HRS 89-11(f)(6) makes the comparison a statutory factor.
  • Never predicate the employer's ability to pay on raising taxes, fees, or charges. HRS 89-11(f)(4) forbids it.
  • Use 6.5 years as the survey median, never "about 6."
  • Always name the cost of living index being used. Never "lowest in the nation."
  • Educate, do not demand. Present the data and the mechanism, and let the reader draw the conclusion.
  • No use of the August 2023 fire as a compensation argument. Official findings may be cited factually and nothing more.
  • Say "wage adjustment," not "raise." It tracks inflation, so it is not a gain.

Structural

Without that, this plan dead-ends in a filing cabinet in the fall of 2028 having done everything right.
